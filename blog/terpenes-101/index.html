Meet the terpenes

Terpenes are aromatic compounds found in cannabis and in plenty of other plants. The same names turn up in mango, hops, black pepper and lavender, which is why the vocabulary can sound like a spice rack.

Cannabinoids such as THC, CBD, CBG and CBN matter, and terpenes matter too. Neither tells the whole story alone. How any product feels varies from person to person, so think of terpenes as part of a product's profile, not a promise about your night.

If you've only ever shopped by THC number, this is the next layer. It's also a much more fun conversation to have at the counter, and it gives you words for what you liked last time.

You don't need a chemistry degree. You need a little vocabulary and a willingness to ask.

Terpenes on our menu

Some product descriptions on our menu name their terpenes. On the Nov 5, 2026 snapshot, 147 descriptions mentioned terpenes, and the names that came up most were limonene (28 descriptions), caryophyllene (27) and myrcene (22).

Terpinolene (8), pinene (7) and linalool (6) showed up too, just less often. If one of those is your thing, it's worth asking which products list it.

Product names give hints, too, but names aren't lab results. A name like Lemon Cherry Gelato tells you what the brand called it. The description, and the conversation at the counter, tell you more.

Want to dig in? Browse flower, pre-rolls and carts and open the descriptions. The terpene talk tends to live there.

Ask us at the counter

This is where the counter earns its keep. Ask which terpenes a product lists, how the brand describes it, and what else on the menu is similar. As our About Us page puts it: “Our knowledgeable staff is here to guide you through our extensive menu, providing personalized recommendations and ensuring that you find the perfect product for your needs.”

One Google review, from Angel Makeda, names the conversation first: “The conversation, explaining what the flowers are and what they do!” That's the idea. Come with questions, leave with answers.

Bring the name of a product you liked last time. It's an easy way to find its cousins on the shelf. New to all of this? Start with what the (S), (I) and (H) mean and how to read a label, then come talk terpenes.

Talk terpenes like a regular

  • Ask which terpenes a product lists
  • Note the terpenes on products you enjoy, so you can ask for more like them
  • Read descriptions, not just names
  • Bring the name of a product you liked last time
  • Remember that effects vary from person to person
  • Start low, go slow with anything new

Questions, answered

What are terpenes?

Aromatic compounds found in cannabis and other plants, like myrcene, limonene, caryophyllene, pinene, linalool and terpinolene.

Does your menu list terpenes?

Some product descriptions name them. On Nov 5, 2026, 147 descriptions on our menu mentioned terpenes.

Do terpenes change how cannabis feels?

Cannabinoids and terpenes both matter, and how a product feels varies from person to person. That's why it helps to note the terpenes on products you enjoy.

Are terpenes only in cannabis?

No. They're aromatic compounds found in other plants too: myrcene in mango and hops, caryophyllene in black pepper, linalool in lavender.

Which terpenes show up most on your menu?

On Nov 5, 2026, limonene (28 descriptions), caryophyllene (27) and myrcene (22) were mentioned most.

Can you recommend a product by terpene?

Ask at the counter. Our About Us page promises “personalized recommendations,” and terpenes are a good place to start that conversation.

Which terpene smells like citrus?

Limonene is often described as citrus. Myrcene is earthy, caryophyllene peppery, pinene like pine, linalool floral, and terpinolene fresh and herbal.
