What the (S), (I) and (H) mean

Scroll our menu and you'll spot a letter in parentheses after a lot of product names. Here's what it tells you, what it doesn't, and what to read next.

Three letters

On the Two Buds menu, a letter after the product name marks how it's classified: (S) for sativa, (I) for indica and (H) for hybrid. One Brass Knuckles all-in-one reads East Coast Sour Diesel (S). A Brass Knuckles 510 cart reads Skywalker OG (I). Another reads Guava Gelato (H).

On the Nov 5, 2026 snapshot, 899 of our 1,150 products carried a classification. Most of the 251 without one are accessories (82) and edibles (98). Edibles can carry a letter too: 131 of our 229 edibles did.

Ancestry, not a promise

Here's the part a lot of menus skip. Indica, sativa and hybrid describe plant ancestry and shape more reliably than they describe effects. Two products with the same letter can land differently, and how any product feels varies from person to person.

So treat the letter as a starting point. It's one clue about a plant's family tree, not a forecast for your evening. The rest of the story is in the numbers and the chemistry.

Think of it like a last name. It tells you something about the family. It doesn't tell you what the person is like. Use it alongside the numbers on the listing and the terpenes in the description.

Same name, different letter

The proof is on our own shelves. Lemon Cherry Gelato shows up on eight tagged listings from eight different brands: four say indica and four say hybrid. Blue Dream is tagged sativa on 14 listings and hybrid on 6. Runtz carries all three letters, depending on the product.

Gelato names carry all three letters too. Some names do agree with themselves: all 15 listings with Sour Diesel in the name are tagged sativa.

Here's why that matters. The tag belongs to each listing, so read it as a note about that product, not as a rule for the strain name. Want to see how a name spreads across our menu? The strain library tracks twelve of them.

Read past the letter

The listing has more to say. THC shows as a percentage on flower, pre-rolls, carts and concentrates. Some names spell out the whole cannabinoid mix, like Ayrloom's 1.0g AIO vapes with ratios such as 600THC:150CBD:150CBG in the name.

Cannabinoids such as THC, CBD, CBG and CBN matter, and so do terpenes, the aromatic compounds also found in other plants. Start with Terpenes 101 and how to read a label, then shop flower with fresh eyes.

Format changes the numbers too. The same strain name can come as flower, a pre-roll, a cart or a concentrate. Most flower on our Nov 5 menu listed between 20% and 33% THC, while most concentrates listed more than 50%.

If you remember one thing from this page, make it this: the letter starts the conversation. It doesn't finish it. Ask us at the counter.

Questions, answered

Does indica always mean relaxed?

No. Indica, sativa and hybrid describe plant ancestry and shape more reliably than effects, and how a product feels varies from person to person.

What does (H) mean on your menu?

(H) marks a product classified as a hybrid, a plant with mixed indica and sativa ancestry. On Nov 5, 2026 it was the most common tag on our menu, with 380 products.

Is sativa or indica stronger?

Neither letter tells you strength. Check the THC on the listing, and remember that how a product feels varies from person to person.

Why do some products have no letter?

Most untagged items on our menu are accessories and edibles. If you want to know more about any product, ask us at the counter.

Do pre-rolls and carts carry tags too?

Many do. The (S), (I) and (H) show up across flower, pre-rolls, carts and concentrates on our menu, wherever the listing carries a classification.

Where can I see how one strain is tagged across the menu?

Our strain library tracks twelve names, from Blue Dream to Jack Herer, with the tags and the products for each.

Where should I start if I'm new?

Start low, go slow, and ask questions. Our first visit guide covers the basics.
