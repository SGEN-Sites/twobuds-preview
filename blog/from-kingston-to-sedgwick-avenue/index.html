One party, one rec room

Hip-hop's birth is widely traced to a back-to-school party on August 11, 1973. The DJ was Kool Herc, born in Kingston, Jamaica. The room was the rec room of 1520 Sedgwick Avenue in the Bronx.

You know what came after, or you think you do. This post is about what came before: an island tradition that crossed the water and found a new home in the Bronx.

Every big story has a small room in it somewhere. This one has a rec room, a back-to-school crowd and a DJ who brought his own way of doing things.

The sound system

Herc brought Jamaican sound-system style to Bronx parties: big speaker rigs and extended breaks. Big rigs, so the music filled the room. Extended breaks, so the break of a record kept going.

That's the thread. A Jamaican tradition, carried to a new borough, turned into something new. Kingston on one end, Sedgwick Avenue on the other, and a party in between.

It's a reminder of what people carry with them when they arrive somewhere new. Sometimes it's a recipe. Sometimes it's a speaker stack.

Think about what that takes: gear, timing and a feel for the room. It's craft, the same way knowing a menu well is craft.

Island roots, Bronx address

Two Buds was founded by an entrepreneur with Jamaican roots, and the shop wears it with pride. Our About Us page describes “a warm and inviting atmosphere,” and our homepage description promises “a warm, Jamaican vibe.”

You can hear the accent in the deals, too. A pre-roll deal on the Nov 5, 2026 menu was called Duppy ReMix. A gummy deal opened with FOOL FOOL. And the Two Buds house line in Accessories includes a Jamaican Ashtray.

The Duppy ReMix deal, a “15% Off Special,” covered pre-rolls from Jetpacks, Kushy Punch, Cru, Ritual, Hurley Grown and Toke Folks on Nov 5. A remix, in pre-roll form.

None of that is a costume. It's part of who opened the doors at 696 East 241 Street. Our About Us page calls the shop “a community hub, a cultural oasis,” and we mean both.

The borough keeps the beat

Music history runs all through this borough. Woodlawn Cemetery, historic in its own right, is where Duke Ellington and Miles Davis are buried. Plan a day around it with our Bronx day out.

And if you want the rest of the story, start with the place itself. 1520 Sedgwick Avenue is a Bronx address, same as ours.

Hip-hop's story is widely traced to one Bronx address. Two Buds has another. Between them sits a lot of Bronx history, and all of it is worth knowing.

Questions, answered

Where was hip-hop born?

Its birth is widely traced to a back-to-school party DJ Kool Herc threw on August 11, 1973 in the rec room of 1520 Sedgwick Avenue in the Bronx.

What did Kool Herc bring to Bronx parties?

Jamaican sound-system style: big speaker rigs and extended breaks.

Who was Kool Herc?

A DJ born in Kingston, Jamaica. Hip-hop's birth is widely traced to the back-to-school party he threw on August 11, 1973 at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue.

Does Two Buds have Jamaican roots?

Yes. Two Buds was founded by an entrepreneur with Jamaican roots, and our About Us page says Two Buds “embodies the rich culture and tradition of Jamaica’s cannabis heritage.”

Where is Two Buds?

696 East 241 Street, Bronx, NY 10470, in Wakefield. The closest subway is Wakefield-241st Street on the 2 train.
